All features Sprite baker

3D to 2D sprite baker for Unity

Sun Forge bakes a 3D model and its animations into 2D sprite sheets, with lighting textures, shadows and the prefab that plays them.

Directions

Point at a sprite or tap it, or drag the slider, to turn the model. The sprite of the direction it faces plays with it.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek, CC BY 4.0, modified.

Directions and camera angle

8-directional sprites, or any count from 1 to 64

A bake writes 1 to 64 directions, such as 4, 8 or 16, and you switch off any you do not need. The camera looks from any angle between 0 and 90 degrees, in steps of 0.25: a side view, straight down, and every angle between, isometric included.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek, CC BY 4.0, modified.

Animations

3D animations to 2D sprite sheets

Pick the animation clips to bake. The bake writes a sprite sheet for each direction, the animation clips, an Animator Controller and the prefab that plays them. These are standard Unity assets, and no 3D model, mesh or rig reaches your build.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek, CC BY 4.0, modified.

What a bake writes

Normal maps and lighting textures

Each sheet has a normal, a mask and a Geometry texture beside its color, and an emission texture when its bake has Emission on. A bake writes only the textures you switch on.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek, CC BY 4.0, modified.

What a bake writes

Emission textures that light the scene

A bake writes glowing parts to an emission texture. With Glow Light on it also adds a light at the glow, which lights the ground and the sprites near it. Drag the star to compare.

Prefabs and archetypes

A prefab with a collider and a hitbox

A baked prefab has the parts its archetype gives it, such as a collider, a hitbox, a health bar, a VFX box and an overhead anchor, which you drag in the Scene view.

The Hero, Enemy and Wildlife archetypes add behaviors: the Hero walks and shoots, the Enemy attacks up close, and the Wildlife wanders. You write your own behaviors in C# and add them to an archetype.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek, CC BY 4.0, modified.

The Bake Window

Preview a bake before it runs

The Bake Window shows the 3D model, the bake camera and the sprite a bake will write, in a preview you orbit, with markers you drag into place.

Baking runs in the editor, from the Bake Window or from editor code.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek, CC BY 4.0, modified.

The Bake Window

Presets, archetypes and batch baking

A preset keeps how one model bakes, and an archetype holds the settings every bake of one kind shares, such as its output folder, camera and shadows. The Batch tab lists every saved preset and bakes the ones you select in one run.

Shown with the showcase's models.

The sprite baker

What else a bake does

Characters, props, particles and tiles

A model or a prefab bakes with its animation clips and its particle systems, in its own materials, Shader Graphs included. Props, particle effects such as a campfire's flame, and ground tiles bake too.

Soft shadows for sprites

A bake also writes soft ground-projected or ellipse shadows, particle flipbooks and sprite atlases. A shadow keeps the sun direction it was baked with.

Rebakes keep your prefab

A rebake keeps the prefab's GUID, the children and components you added to it, your platform overrides, and the collider, hitbox and health bar you adjusted in the Scene view.

Bake time and texture memory

The showcase's beech tree, in 16 directions, bakes in about 25 seconds on a Ryzen 9 7900X with an RTX 4060 and takes 9.5 MiB of texture memory, packed for Windows, Mac and Linux. Its knight, 968 sprites of 4 animations, bakes in about a minute and takes 81 MiB.

Addressables and editor code

A bake can register its prefab and atlases with Addressables. You can bake from editor code and extend the bake with your own C# steps.

Sprites without the lighting

A bake can write plain sprites and give its prefab Unity's default sprite material, so the baker works without Sun Forge's lighting.

Light the sprites you bake

One purchase holds the sprite baker and Sun Forge 2D Lighting, which reads the textures a bake writes. See all features, or read the guide.
