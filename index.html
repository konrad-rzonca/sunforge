In your game

Every character and prop here is a 2D sprite

Each one is a flat sprite baked from a 3D model. The sun and the fires light them live.

Sun Forge is a 3D to 2D sprite baker and real-time 2D lighting for Unity 6 and URP. Use them together, or either one alone.

Bake a 3D model into a 2D character

Pick the directions and the animations. Sun Forge renders every frame as a sprite and builds the prefab that plays them.

Directions

Point at a sprite or tap it, or drag the slider, to turn the model. The sprite of the direction it faces plays with it.

Sprites light like 3D models

Metal shines, creases darken, and baked sprites cast shadows on each other. A bake writes the textures the light reads, and you paint none of them. Drag the star to take the light off the hall, and pick a texture to see each one.

For drawn sprites

Light the sprites you already have

Run the Set Up for Lighting command on your sprites, pixel art too. A normal map you bring gives them depth. Pick which colors are metal and which glow, and Sun Forge writes the metal and glow textures.

For platformers

Bake from the side for a platformer

At Elevation Angle 0 your models bake as side-view sprites. Light the level with a sun you move, lights you place and a style you tune.

For pixel art, bake at a low Pixels Per Unit with point filtering.

The characters, plants, chest and torch parts come from the showcase sample.

For isometric games

Bake for isometric games

Keep the Elevation Angle at 30 and a square of ground draws as a 2:1 diamond. Stack blocks in levels, or paint one level on a Tilemap of baked tiles.

The characters, props and plants come from the showcase sample.

Pick a lighting style and every sprite relights

Regular, Cinematic and Flat light the same sprites, with nothing baked again. Save one as your own and tune it.

Questions

Before you buy

Do I rebake when I move a light?

No. The sun, the lights and the style change live. Shadows move with their sprites and keep the direction they were baked with.

Are the sprites locked to Sun Forge?

No. A bake writes standard Unity sprites, atlases, animation clips, an Animator Controller and a prefab.

Do my 3D models end up in the game?

No. No 3D model, mesh or rig goes into your build.

Does it fit a 3D game?

If your 3D game keeps one camera angle, bake its models into sprites and build it in 2D.

Can I light sprites I drew myself?

Yes. Select your sprites, right-click and choose Sun Forge > Set Up for Lighting. A normal map of your own gives them form.

Where does it run?

It is built for every platform URP supports. Builds of your game run on Windows with Direct3D 12, on ARM64 Android with Vulkan or OpenGL ES 3, and on the web with WebGL 2. On Unity 6.6, web builds run with WebGPU too.

Questions

More questions

Can I use the 2D lighting without the sprite baker?

Yes. Each works without the other. The lighting lights sprites you drew in any 2D or 2.5D scene, and a bake can write plain sprites for Unity's default sprite material.

Does it work with Unity's Light 2D?

Yes. A Light 2D can drive a Sun Forge Light Source, and it keeps lighting Sun Forge's Unity Lights material.

How long does a bake take?

On a Ryzen 9 7900X with an RTX 4060, a tree in 16 directions bakes in about 25 seconds, and a 4-animation character, 968 sprites, in about a minute.

How much texture memory do baked sprites take?

On desktop, the showcase's beech tree in 16 directions takes 9.5 MiB, and its knight's 968 sprites 81 MiB. Fewer directions, a lower resolution or fewer lighting textures take less.

What can I bake?

A model or a prefab with its animation clips and particle systems, in its own materials and Shader Graphs. Props, particle effects and ground tiles bake too.

Can I bake from code, or use Addressables?

Yes. You can bake from editor code and extend the bake with your own C# steps, and a bake can register its prefab and atlases with Addressables.
