Lighting

The Sun Forge Gizmos panel in the Scene view has a row for each thing it shows. Lights draws how far each light reaches on the ground, Shadow Height how high a flat sprite such as a rug casts its shadow from, and Feet Line where a sprite you drew stands. Every row starts off. Turn one on to see its marks and to drag the handles of what you select. The rows draw nothing while the Scene view's Gizmos is off, and the panel says so. A row shows only while the scene has something for it, and the panel hides while it has no row to show. To hide it for good, use the Scene view's Overlays menu. The crosshair beside Lighting pings the scene's Lighting Setup in the Hierarchy, and Texture View at the foot of the panel shows a texture from each sprite (Sprite Textures).

Light Source

A Light Source lights the styled materials under both URP renderers. Create one with GameObject > Sun Forge > Light Source > Light, or Spot Light, Line Light or Rectangle Light for a cone, a line or a rectangle of light, and move it to where the light shows, such as onto a lamp's flame. For a starting look, such as a torch, a street lamp or a window, open Fire, Lamps, Magic or Windows and Doors in the same submenu. To make an object you already have into a light, such as a lamp prefab, use Add Component > Sun Forge > Lighting > Light Source. Its pool lies on the ground Height Above Ground below it and reaches Ground Radius out, across the ground and across anything raised, and the Scene view's outline stays at ground height. A Ground Radius of 0 lights nothing. Raising a light keeps its Ground Radius and makes the ground right below it dimmer. The ground is evenly lit inside Inner Radius and fades from there to Ground Radius. In Regular and Cinematic, nearby metal reflects the pool, and the pool lights the sides and undersides of standing sprites that face it, tinted by the ground's color, or by the Lighting Setup's Ground Bounce Color where you set one. Ground Bounce Strength in the style's Default Lighting Values turns this light down together with the rest of the light the ground bounces. To save its GPU time at some quality levels or at all of them, set Light From Lit Ground in the style's Quality section (Style Quality).

When you select it, the Scene view draws a line from the light down to the ground and a dashed outline where its light ends there. Drag the dot above the light to set Height Above Ground: the light rises or sinks and its pool stays where it is. On side-view art, dragging it sets how far in front of the art the light stands, and the light stays where it is. The other handles set Ground Radius, Inner Radius, the size and the spot angles. Point at a handle to see its name and value. With Lights on in the Sun Forge Gizmos panel, selecting a prefab also shows the handles of its lights, such as a candle's flame.

Each light shows a bulb in the Scene view in its color, and a light a Light 2D drives shows the Light 2D's icon. A camera lights up to 512 lights, or 128 in a WebGL 2 player, and a gray bulb marks a light the main camera can see but leaves out because more reach its view. Its Inspector says why, and whether Is Prioritized under Special Cases keeps it lit. While the build target is WebGL with WebGL 2 in its Graphics APIs, the Scene view and the bulbs show what a WebGL 2 player lights.

To make a spot, lower Outer Spot Angle. Its handles then show in the Scene view. Drag the Aim handle, the dot with an arc past the pool's edge, to aim it. It turns the GameObject.

To light along a line or over a rectangle, such as a tube lamp or a glowing panel, set Shape. Shape Length runs along the GameObject's up axis, which the Aim handle turns.

Glow Renderers lists the renderers that draw a light's glow. The light does not light the glowing particles in the list, such as a flame's, so they show their own glow, and every other light still lights them. For this light to light them, take them out of the list. A Flame then stops flickering their glow.

To make a light flicker like fire, set Kind to Flame. In Play mode the glow of each renderer in Glow Renderers flickers with it. For a flame sprite of your own, add its renderer to the list. When the light is a child of that sprite, the button below the list adds it for you.

A sprite Sun Forge rendered with a Glow light, and a sprite whose textures you wrote with Write Textures, keep in their emission texture the light their own glowing parts send them. On a sprite whose textures you wrote with Write Textures, the faces its normal texture turns toward the glowing parts take more of that light, and the faces turned away take less. The lighting style sets how such a sprite's Glow light lights the sprite itself, and the Glow light's Light On Its Object Shift and Glow Parts Light Scale, under Special Cases, change that for that sprite alone. Glow Light On Its Object is how much the light lights it as it lights the things around it. Glow Parts Light On Its Object is how strongly the light kept in the texture shows. A flame sprite of your own is lit by its Light Source as any other sprite is.

A sprite with a Regular, Cinematic or Flat material, or a material made from one, takes its Glow light as its material's style sets it when the scene's style is another of the three, such as a Flat material in a Regular scene. The Lighting Setup's two Glow values then do not change it. For this, Realistic counts as Regular, and a style of your own counts as the style you made it from. Every sprite in a scene with no Lighting Setup takes its Glow light the same way. The Glow light's Light On Its Object Shift and Glow Parts Light Scale still change such a sprite, except Glow Parts Light Scale on a Cinematic material, because Cinematic shows none of the light kept in the texture.

Your Light 2D lights keep lighting the Unity-Lit material. To light your styled sprites with them too:

  1. Choose GameObject > Sun Forge > Set Up for Lighting and click Apply. Each Light 2D that targets every sorting layer gets a Light Source that it drives, so scripts and animations that change the Light 2D change the Light Source too.
  2. For a Light 2D that leaves a sorting layer out, select the Lighting Setup and click Fix beside the finding that names the light.
  3. Set each Light Source's Height Above Ground to how high its Light 2D hangs above the ground. The light stays at the Light 2D. The Light 2D lights the Unity-Lit material around itself. On styled sprites it lights the ground below it, so its pool looks flattened, like the ground in your art. On side-view art, set a small Height instead: the light stands that far in front of the art, and its pool lies around it.

Materials

The package has four styles: Regular, Cinematic, Flat and Realistic. Realistic is for art that should look lifelike. It shades as Regular does, with no rim on sunlit edges and no widened sun glint, under a hazy day's sun and sky. To change a scene's style, select its Lighting Setup and click a style under Style.

Each style has an Indoor variant, for a room. It keeps the style's materials and post-processing. Its sun is the room's main light, its sky is dim and warm, and it has no clouds. To light a room with one:

  1. Select the scene's Lighting Setup. Under Style, click Indoor, then the style you want.
  2. For each lamp, fire or window, choose a starting look under GameObject > Sun Forge > Light Source, such as Lamps > Lantern, Fire > Fireplace or Windows and Doors > Window.
  3. Under Regular, Cinematic or Realistic, to have metal reflect your room, import a panorama of it with Texture Shape set to Cube, and assign it as the Lighting Setup's Environment. With none, metal reflects the dim, warm sky. Under Flat, metal reflects no image and no sky.

Flat's Indoor is dimmer than the other styles' Indoor, so that its Light Sources brighten the art around them. Under a higher Sun Intensity they only tint it. For a Flat room of your own, set the Lighting Setup's Light Sources In Sunlight to 1 and lower Sun Intensity.

Every style has one material per role:

Role For Material
Sprite Actors, props and effects that stand up SunForge_<Style>_SpriteLit
Ground Terrain, roads, decals and other flat art drawn top-down, and a side view's floors and walls SunForge_<Style>_Ground
Ground Clutter Stones, tufts, grass and other small solids lying on the ground, drawn from the same angle as your standing sprites SunForge_<Style>_GroundClutter

A sprite whose Lighting Style Renderer names a Material draws that material under every style. A sprite that names none draws the material of the Lighting Setup's style for its Role, so it changes when the style does.

Comes from What
The scene's Lighting Setup Sun, sky and weather, the lights, cast shadows, mist and the post-processing
The sprite's material How the sprite is shaded, and its properties (Tuning A Style)

The Unity-Lit material, SunForge_URP_SpriteLit, belongs to no style. Unity's 2D lights light it under the 2D Renderer, and under the Universal Renderer URP's lights light it as an upright sprite in a scene whose up is +Y, such as a side view or a 3D scene. It reads a sprite's normal, mask and emission textures, and takes no mist, cast shadows or ground cover from the Lighting Setup.

Tuning A Style

To tune a look, save it as a style of your own:

  1. Select your Lighting Setup and click Save As New Style. The Lighting Setup takes the new style and looks the same. The style has its own materials and post-processing profile, saved beside it. Under Style, the Lighting Setup shows what every Lighting Setup on the style shares: its Look, post-processing, Cloud And Mist Texture and Quality.
  2. In Look, set the rows. Each row sets the style's Sprite, Ground and Ground Clutter materials together.
  3. To tune the post-processing, edit the overrides under Post Processing Profile.

For a style that starts as Regular, choose Assets > Create > Sun Forge > Lighting Style instead.

The Lighting Setup's Values hold the sun, sky and weather, and under Glow how a Glow light lights the object it belongs to. Under Regular, Glow Light On Its Object at 0 with Glow Parts Light On Its Object at 1 lights the object by what its glowing parts send it. Regular sets Glow Parts Light On Its Object higher, which brightens that light.

A dimmed row does nothing in the scene as it is now, such as a night row by day. Its tooltip says why. Your edit is kept.

Realistic's materials are Regular's with other values, so a row listed for Regular applies to Realistic too. Realistic has no Pixel Outline material.

Each row's tooltip says what it does. Some rows need more:

Property Styles Note
Night Vision Strength All On Flat, start at 0.5
Highlight Ceiling Regular, Cinematic Red, green and blue each level off on their own, so a bright firelit glint turns from orange to gold to yellow. Without tonemapping, as under Regular, a glint above 1 turns to flat color
Pixel Outline, Pixel Outline Color Regular, Cinematic The ring draws only inside the sprite's rectangle, so leave a transparent pixel around the art, and on a sprite whose Mesh Type is Tight keep Extrude Edges at 1 or more. To outline only some sprites, set the Material of their Lighting Style Renderer to SunForge_Regular_SpriteLit_PixelOutline or SunForge_Cinematic_SpriteLit_PixelOutline, the shipped Sprite material with Pixel Outline on
Outline Width (World Units) Flat The default draws one sprite pixel at 64 Pixels Per Unit, and one and a half at 96. A sprite scaled down keeps the width, so a small copy looks more heavily outlined. Crease lines stay one sprite pixel wide
Light And Shade Color Flat Start at 0.5
Receives Cast Shadows, Bake Camera Elevation (Degrees), Height Range (World Units), Shadow Sun Elevation (Degrees), Shadow Sun Direction (Degrees) All Not in Look: select your style and click Open beside a material to set them on that material. The Lighting Setup's Art View outranks Bake Camera Elevation (Degrees)
Emission Scale All Not in Look: set it on each material, as above. It also brightens the Glow light that Write Textures adds (Glow, Surfaces and Edges, on a sprite's Lighting Style Renderer)

Leave Flat On Ground and Uses Top-Down Normal Map as they are: they match the material's role.

Leave two transparent pixels around each sprite, so two sprites in one texture are four pixels apart. Where a sprite has fewer, Flat's outline and the rims of Regular and Cinematic are missing along that edge.

The style's post-processing is its Volume profile, SunForge_<Style>_PostProcessing. The Lighting Setup applies it at priority 0.5. A Volume of your own with a priority below 0.5 sets what the profile leaves out, such as a vignette on Flat. One at 1 or more overrides the style. Regular and Flat turn tonemapping off, so a tonemapper set for the whole project does not show under them. Set it on a Volume with a priority of 1 or more instead. Realistic's profile sets Tonemapping to Neutral, with Regular's vignette and no change to contrast or saturation. Its bloom starts at a higher brightness than the other styles', so a glow blooms and a sunlit wall barely does. The styles are tuned for the URP asset's Grading Mode at Low Dynamic Range, its default. At High Dynamic Range, which HDR display output also turns on, their nights are darker, and under Cinematic a fire's bright core turns a flat yellow. To turn the style's post-processing off, clear Post Processing Enabled on the Lighting Setup.

Time Of Day

Time Of Day, under Style on the Lighting Setup, sets the hour, from 0 to 24. Each value follows the style at that hour. The marks on the slider show the hours the style keys.

The style's Hours hold its keys. 12:00 is always its Default Lighting Values, and each other key sets some values at its hour. Between keys, a value blends from the last key that sets it to the next one. Regular, Cinematic, Flat and Realistic key dawn, morning, afternoon, dusk and a night from 20:00 to 04:00. An Indoor style keys only 12:00. To change them:

  1. Select your Lighting Setup. If it uses one of Sun Forge's styles, click Save As New Style.
  2. Select the style and open Hours. Each key lists every value with a checkbox in front of it. Select a value's checkbox to set it at that hour.
  3. Click Add Hour for a new key. Turn on Held for a key that holds its values over a span of hours.

The values an hour usually sets:

Value What it changes
Sun Elevation Which sides the sun lights, and how bright level ground is
Sun Color, Sun Intensity, Sun Warmth The sunlight's color and brightness
Ambient Sky Color, Ambient Sky Intensity The light from the sky, in shade and on every side
Night, and the moon's values Night fades the sun into the moon and turns materials to night vision
Cast Shadow Stretch Enabled and the values under it Longer ground shadows, as at dusk
Ground Shadow Strength How dark shadow sprites and a Cast Shadow Field's ground overlay are
Mist Enabled and the values under it Ground mist

A value you set on the Lighting Setup keeps your value at every hour. The revert button on its row, or Revert All, gives it back to the hours. A line under the values names the ones you set that the style's hours would change.

To run a day from a script, set SunForgeLightingSetup.TimeOfDay in Update. The Lighting Setup shows that hour in the same frame.

Style Quality

A style's Quality section sets which costly parts of the lighting draw at each quality level. The shipped styles draw each Standard option at every level and no Extra option. To change that:

  1. Select your Lighting Setup. If it uses one of Sun Forge's styles, click Save As New Style.
  2. Select the style. Its Quality section lists each option.
  3. Beside the option, pick Every Level, Never, or From and a level. From High, for example, draws the option at High and at each level listed after it in Project Settings > Quality, and leaves it out at the levels listed before it.
Option Group Styles Effect
Light From Lit Ground Standard Regular, Cinematic Whether a Light Source's pool on the ground lights the sides and undersides of standing sprites that face it, and nearby metal reflects it
Exact Shadows On Sprites Extra Every style Whether a shadow on a sprite stays correct where shadows overlap or its caster stands close to the sprite, where it otherwise fades or shows an edge. It needs a Cast Shadow Field that casts on sprites

When your game changes its quality level, the lighting follows on the next frame. A style that leaves a Standard option out or draws an Extra one at some level adds shader variants to your builds, and a style that does neither adds none. If you build Addressables or asset bundles, clear their build cache after you change a style's Quality.

Performance

A setting that costs frame time shows a gauge after its name. The needle points left for a small cost, up for a medium one and right for a large one, and the tooltip says what it costs. The Lighting Setup's Performance section lists what costs in the open scenes, largest first, with Select beside each line. A setting that costs nothing as it is set, such as a switch that is off, is dimmed at the end.

To set the costly settings at once, open Performance and click Fastest, Balanced or Full, then tune any setting from there:

Button Mist Enabled Cast Shadow Stretch Enabled Post Processing Enabled Cast Shadow Field Sorted Ground Cover
Fastest Off Off Off At most a quarter of the art's resolution, ground only Disabled
Balanced Off Back to the style's, if off On Half the art's resolution, sprites too Enabled
Full Back to the style's, if off Back to the style's, if off On At least the art's resolution, sprites too Enabled

Each button leaves the style, the Light Sources and Unity's Render Scale as they are. Undo takes its changes back in one step.

Sprite Textures

To give a sprite you drew its textures:

  • Bring your own. Save each as a PNG beside the sprite's texture, named as in the table, where <sheet> is that texture's file name without its extension. Then run Set Up for Lighting, or click Change Setup on the sprite's Lighting Style Renderer. Under Sprites, the sprite's row names each file it found, and Apply gives it to the sprite. A sprite that already has a texture of that kind keeps it.
  • Have Sun Forge write them. Once Set Up for Lighting has set the sprite up, select its object and open Glow, Surfaces and Edges on its Lighting Style Renderer. Pick the colors that glow under Glow Colors and the ones that shine like metal under Surface Colors, and turn on Round Edges for rounded edges. Then click Write Textures.

You can also bind them under Secondary Textures in Unity's Sprite Editor. A style's material reads them from any sprite that has them:

Texture File Secondary texture name Channels
Normal <sheet>_Normal.png _NormalMap On Ground in a scene drawn from above, a normal map drawn top-down. Otherwise, one drawn from the same angle as your standing sprites. Fill its texels outside the sprite with the flat normal, 128, 128, 255, or the sprite's edge draws dark. Read it with SFUnpackNormal from SunForgeNormalDecode.hlsl, or the Sun Forge Sample Normal Map node in Shader Graph. Both read 128 as level
Mask <sheet>_MaskTex.png _MaskTex R metallic, G smoothness, B ambient occlusion, and on a sprite Sun Forge rendered, A how much sun reaches it. Linear
Emission <sheet>_Emission.png _EmissionTex The glow's color at intensity 1, sRGB, and on a texture Sun Forge rendered or wrote, A how much light the sprite takes from its own glow
Geometry texture <sheet>_Geometry.png _GeometryTex R height above the pivot: black at the pivot, brighter is higher. A is optional (Custom-Shaders.md, Geometry Textures). Linear and uncrunched

A sprite with a mask of another layout under _MaskTex lights wrong.

To see these textures on your sprites, turn on Texture View at the foot of the Sun Forge Gizmos panel and pick one. It shows in that Scene view only and changes nothing in the scene. Shaded on the note at the top of the view goes back to the scene. A view shows only what a lit or unlit URP material draws, so sprites under Sprites/Default, cast shadows, lines and text hide. A sprite that 2D Animation deforms on the GPU draws in its bind pose. A Sprite Mask with Unity's built-in material masks nothing, so sprites set to Visible Inside Mask hide. The note says when either applies, and Use URP Material on it gives those masks URP's material.

  • Under Normal, turn on Flag Missing to outline each sprite with no normal map of its own.
  • Under Geometry, the colors run to the tallest height the view draws, and the scale in the note gives that height in units. They settle when the view stops moving. A texel with no height shows in dim gray.
  • Under Emission, a sprite with no emission shows in dim gray.

Painting A Geometry Texture

A sprite that stands upright needs no Geometry texture. Paint one for a sprite that is not an upright card, such as a table top, a cart or a figure lying down.

  1. Paint gray over every pixel the sprite covers: black at the ground, brighter the higher it stands. On a sheet, leave a sprite transparent to keep it an upright card.
  2. Put the sprite's pivot on its front edge where it meets the ground, such as a cart's front wheels. Grass draws over the sprite only in front of the pivot. For a sprite that stands on another, such as a lamp on a table, put the pivot where it meets the table, and set Height Above Ground on its Lighting Style Renderer to the table's height.
  3. Export a PNG named <sheet>_Geometry.png beside the sheet.
  4. Run Set Up for Lighting. The sprite's row names the texture, and Apply imports it and gives it to the sprite.
  • To work in pixels, gray is height in pixels * 255 / (Pixels Per Unit * range), where range is the material's Height Range (World Units). At 32 Pixels Per Unit and the default range of 8, one gray step is about one pixel.
  • A part that stands straight up, such as a chimney, and is N pixels tall in the drawing is N / cos(elevation) pixels high, where elevation is the angle of the Lighting Setup's Art View.
  • With Point filtering, nothing around the sprite matters. A filtered sheet needs the export's background transparent. Anything you leave transparent needs a PNG with alpha.

Cast Shadows

  • Set Up for Lighting sets the Sprite Sort Point of each sprite it sets up as Standing to Pivot, so standing sprites that share a Sorting Layer and an Order in Layer sort by Y from their feet. A sprite in a Sorting Group keeps its sort point.
  • A Cast Shadow Field follows one camera, so in split screen both views share the shadows of the camera it follows.
  • In a dense crowd of hundreds of casters, a small part of a sprite can stay lit where a neighbor's shadow falls on it. The Cast Shadow Field's Inspector says when that can happen, and what to do.
  • A caster whose shadow is far from the camera's view costs almost nothing. One whose Sprite Renderers are turned off costs a check of each of them every frame.
  • The Cast Shadow Field draws once a frame, before the first camera. If a script moves the camera or a sprite between two renders in one frame, as a capture does, call its Redraw before the later render.
  • A Scale X of -1 on a parent, which turns a character to face left, does not mirror a paired shadow, so it still falls away from the sun. Outside a Cast Shadow Field, a shadow sprite whose shader is not Sun Forge/Hand-Drawn Shadow mirrors with its parent.
  • A shadow sprite is as dark as you drew it under the Regular style's daylight with no cloud over it. It is darker under a sun stronger against its sky, as in the Realistic style, and fades as the sun or moon weakens against the sky, under cloud and under a lower sun. A higher Ground Shadow Strength on the Lighting Setup darkens it. It also fades inside a Light Source's light, except on the Flat look and in a side view. Outside a Cast Shadow Field, a shadow sprite whose shader is not Sun Forge/Hand-Drawn Shadow keeps its darkness.

A sprite that does not receive cast shadows also takes no mist and stands in no ground cover.

The mist also does not cover particles, shadow sprites whose shader is not Sun Forge/Hand-Drawn Shadow, or shaders of your own that do not light through Sun Forge Lit Color.

Canopy Shade

A Canopy Shade takes the sky's light off the ground and the sprites under tree crowns. To set one up:

  1. Choose GameObject > Sun Forge > Canopy Shade.
  2. Put your trees, and nothing else, under one object, with groups inside it if you like, and set the Canopy Shade's Trees to it.
  3. Set Rect to the ground the trees stand on.
  4. Click Draw Map. It saves the map beside the scene and sets Map and Canopy Height.

After you move, add or remove a tree, click Draw Map again. If you changed the map, Draw Map keeps yours and saves a new one.

Run Set Up for Lighting on trees made from your own sprites, or they cast no shade.

Set Canopy Shade Strength on the Lighting Setup to choose how much of the shade shows.

To paint a map instead, cover Rect with it: white is open sky and black is under closed crowns. Draw each crown's shade 1 / sin e times as wide as it is deep, where e is the angle of the Lighting Setup's Art View, which is From Above wherever canopy shade shows. Parts of a sprite above Canopy Height stay lit.

Side Views

Art drawn from the side, such as a platformer's, is a side view. To light a side-view scene:

  1. Choose GameObject > Sun Forge > Set Up for Lighting.
  2. In the window's first row, Art View, pick From the Side, then click Apply. Your sprites, floors and walls then light as a side view, under any style and material.
  3. On the Lighting Setup, set Sun Direction and Sun Elevation so the sun comes from the side.
  4. For a torch or a fire, add a light with GameObject > Sun Forge > Light Source > Fire > Torch. By day its pool shows in Regular and Cinematic. In Flat, a pool shows by day only where the light is much brighter than the sun and sky on that surface, or wherever it reaches once you raise the Lighting Setup's Light Sources In Sunlight. At night, a brighter moon or night sky does not take the pool away. If the sun drowns a pool, raise the light's Intensity, and raise Ground Radius to widen it.

Art View is also on the Lighting Setup, where you can change it later. Each scene's Lighting Setup has its own, so one project can hold side-view and top-down levels.

On side-view art:

  • Set Up for Lighting moves no pivot and adds no Cast Shadow Caster or Cast Shadow Field. It sets each sprite to the Sprite role, and each Tiled sprite, Tilemap and Sprite Shape to Ground, for floors and walls.
  • A Light Source shines from its GameObject and lights the art as it lights the ground from above. Its light reaches Ground Radius across the screen, and Height Above Ground is how far in front of the art it stands: a higher light lights the art more evenly and more dimly. For a lamp on a wall or in a hand, set a small Height, however high it hangs. A light you add from the Light Source menu after step 2 starts at a Height that suits side-view art. Shape and the spot angles lie on the screen.
  • Cloud shadows, canopy shade, mist, ground cover, cast shadows and Light From Lit Ground do not show, because a side view shows no ground for them to lie on.
  • The Ground material lights floors and walls as if they face the camera, like a Sprite, and reads their normal map as a Sprite's.
  • A light lights every side-view sprite within its reach, a backdrop included. To keep a backdrop unlit, add it to the light's Excluded Objects.
  • A sprite at a smaller Z than a light, such as a character walking past a wall torch, is in front of it: the light reaches only the sprite's edges, and in Flat none of the sprite. To light the sprite's front, give the light the sprite's Z or a smaller one.

Pixel Perfect Camera

URP's Pixel Perfect Camera draws each sprite pixel as a block of whole screen pixels. Set its Assets Pixels Per Unit to your sprites' Pixels Per Unit. A sprite at another Pixels Per Unit, or a scaled one, draws pixels of another size.

The camera works under the 2D Renderer and the Universal Renderer. Grid Snapping set to Upscale Render Texture works under the 2D Renderer only.

Grid Snapping Sprites move in steps of Lights, mist and cast shadows change
None Screen pixels From one screen pixel to the next, so they fall smoothly across a sprite pixel
Pixel Snapping Whole sprite pixels From one screen pixel to the next, so they fall smoothly across a sprite pixel
Upscale Render Texture Whole sprite pixels Once per sprite pixel, like the art, because the scene renders at the camera's Reference Resolution and is scaled up

Setting Up From A Script

An editor script can light a scene in a style and do what Fix All does:

using System.Collections.Generic;
using KonradRzonca.SunForge;
using KonradRzonca.SunForge.Editor;
using UnityEditor;
using UnityEditor.SceneManagement;
using UnityEngine;
using UnityEngine.Rendering;

SunForgeSceneLighting.SetUp(SunForgeShippedLightingStyle.Regular);
SunForgeSceneLighting.FixAll();

  • SetUp sets the style of the scene's Lighting Setup, or adds a Lighting Setup in that style to a scene with none, and selects nothing. SetUp(SunForgeShippedLightingStyle.Regular, SunForgeLightingStyleVariant.Indoor) sets the scene up in Regular's Indoor variant.
  • FixAll applies every fix that Fix All applies, except a fix that opens Set Up for Lighting, and opens no window. It returns what is left: what only you can fix, each fix Fix All skips because it changes a choice of yours, and each finding to fix in Set Up for Lighting. Call it once the scene has its sprites, and again after you add more: it adds what they need, such as a Cast Shadow Field for a sprite that casts a shadow. Check returns what is left and changes nothing. Show or log the messages. Their wording changes with the editor's language, so do not compare them.
  • A camera that is already loaded keeps its own sort until its scene loads again, and a Scene view keeps its sort until you open it again. To sort them now, also set transparencySortMode and transparencySortAxis on each loaded camera and on each camera SceneView.GetAllSceneCameras() returns.
  • The changes are in the open scenes and the project's assets in memory. Save both before the script ends, as the last two lines do.

A script can save a style of your own and tune it as Look does (Tuning A Style). This one saves the scene's look and turns Pixel Outline on:

var setup = Object.FindAnyObjectByType<SunForgeLightingSetup>();
SunForgeLightingStyle style = SunForgeSceneLighting.SaveAsNewStyle(setup, "Assets/Lighting/My Style.asset");
IReadOnlyList<Material> materials = SunForgeLightingStyleEditing.EnsureOwnMaterials(style);

SunForgeLightingStyleSetting row = SunForgeLightingStyleSettings.Find("_SFPixelOutline");
foreach (Material material in materials)
{
    if (!material.HasProperty(row.Name))
        continue;

    Undo.RecordObject(material, "Set Pixel Outline");
    material.SetFloat(row.Name, 1f);
    if (row.Keyword != null)
        material.EnableKeyword(row.Keyword);
}

EditorSceneManager.SaveOpenScenes();
AssetDatabase.SaveAssets();
  • SaveAsNewStyle does what Save As New Style does with the path you would choose in its save panel: it saves the style with its own materials and post-processing profile, and returns it. It creates a missing folder. Where a file is at the path, it throws, unless you pass overwrite: true and the file is a lighting style, which it then saves into. To tune a style you saved before, load it with AssetDatabase.LoadAssetAtPath<SunForgeLightingStyle> instead.
  • EnsureOwnMaterials does what Make Copies for This Style does, and returns the style's materials. EnsureOwnProfile does what Make a Copy for This Style does under Post Processing Profile, and returns the profile. Both leave a part that is already the style's own as it is. A shipped style is read-only, so both throw for one.
  • SunForgeLightingStyleSettings.MaterialProperties lists every row with the Name of its property, its Tooltip and its Group. A row's label in Look is the property's description in the material's shader: shader.GetPropertyDescription(shader.FindPropertyIndex(row.Name)).
  • A slider's limits are the shader's, from shader.GetPropertyRangeLimits. SetFloat does not keep a value inside them.
  • A row with a Keyword needs both: set the property, and turn the keyword on with EnableKeyword or off with DisableKeyword. A row with a Switch shows only while the row that name gives is on, as Pixel Outline Color does under Pixel Outline.
  • Undo.RecordObject before each change makes the scene draw it at once. Without it, call Publish on the Lighting Setup.
  • The BakedArt rows are not in Look: set one on the material it is for. Leave the Orientation rows as they are.
