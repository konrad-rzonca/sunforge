Custom Shaders

Light a sprite shader of your own with the scene's style, lights, shadows and mist.

Shader Graph

  1. In a Sprite Unlit graph, add the Sun Forge Lit Color node and connect your color to its Albedo.
  2. Connect the node's Color to Base Color and its Alpha to Alpha.
  3. In the Graph Settings, turn on Disable Color Tint. With it off, the sprite's color multiplies the node's output, so a color with a channel at zero, such as a fade to black, also loses the glow, rim, mist and hit flash in that channel.
  4. On each renderer that uses the material, choose Add Component > Sun Forge > Lighting > Lighting Style Renderer, and set its Material to your material. On a Mesh, Skinned Mesh, Line or Trail Renderer, set your textures on the material, not through a property block of your own, which can make the graph draw nothing.

  • A ground shader, such as water, sets Role to Ground and has a Float property named _SFFlatOnGround at 1, as the ground materials do, so a Cast Shadow Field you add picks the right mode.
  • A sprite drawn above its spot on the ground, such as a bird in flight, is lit at the Height Above Ground of its Lighting Style Renderer. The node reads it, so your graph needs no property for it.
  • A sprite's glow draws at the material's Emission Scale times the sprite's own strength, such as the Emission Multiplier of its Lighting Style Renderer. The node reads that strength, so your graph needs no property for it.
  • A mesh that draws many small sprites at once, such as a shower of coins, lights each one at its own pivot and height. Fill UV channel SunForgeBatchSprites.UvChannel of each sprite's vertices with SunForgeBatchSprites.Vertex. The node and the shipped materials read that channel, so your graph needs nothing for it. Keep your own data out of it. Give the renderer the sprites' textures with SunForgeBatchSprites.SetTextures, which keeps them through style switches.
  • A Sample Texture 2D, DDX, DDY or DDXY node between Color and Base Color can draw wrong colors along the sprite's edges on OpenGL and Vulkan. Sample such a texture with Sample Texture 2D LOD, or feed it to one of the Sun Forge Lit Color node's ports.
  • On a Sprite Skin, run the position plus the Tangent Vector through a second Sprite Skinning node, and connect that result minus the skinned position to the Tangent block, so the relief turns with the bone.
  • On WebGL 2 and OpenGL ES 3.0 a shader can sample 16 textures. The lighting samples 10 of its own, so a shader that samples the sprite's five textures (main, normal, mask, emission and Geometry) can sample one more there.
  • On Direct3D a shader can use 16 sampler states. The lighting uses 6 of its own, so a shader that samples the sprite's five textures through their own samplers can use five more. Textures sampled through one Sampler State node take one state between them.

Hand-Written Shaders

Each include is under Packages/com.konradrzonca.sunforge.lighting/Runtime/Shaders/, and its header says how to use it.

Include Use it to
SunForgeLitColor.hlsl Light a shader as the Sun Forge Lit Color node does.
SunForgeStyleKeywords.hlsl Give the shader a variant for each style, and a variant for each option of a style's Quality section (Lighting.md, Style Quality). Include it with #include_with_pragmas beside the pass's pragmas.
SunForgeNormalDecode.hlsl Read a normal map sample as the shipped materials do, to light with it or to show or measure a normal yourself.
SunForgeHeightDecode.hlsl Declare a Geometry texture, and read the height and front edge of its sample, or the height of a cast height texture's, as the shipped materials do.
SunForgeHeightRamp.hlsl Color a height as the Texture View does under Geometry.
SunForgeHeightRampBar.hlsl Draw the legend's bar for that ramp in a pass named Height Ramp, which SunForgeHeightRamp.DrawBar runs.
SunForgeMist.hlsl Draw the scene's mist, such as in a full-screen fog.
SunForgeHitFlash.hlsl Draw the hit flash.
SunForgeBakedLight.hlsl Read, from an emission texture's alpha, the light a sprite's own glow sends it.
SunForgeWalkBands.hlsl Draw only your shader's piece of a prop that Sun Forge splits around a character.
SunForgeRendererBakeValues.hlsl Read the angle and the other projection values a sprite draws with, and how high it stands above the ground. SFBakeElevationDegrees returns the Lighting Setup's Art View where it has one.
SunForgeLightingGlobals.hlsl Read what the Lighting Setup publishes.

To publish lighting of your own from a script, use SunForgeLightingPublisher.

On WebGPU, Chrome on Windows loads a shader slowly when it reads a static const array at an index computed at run time, or picks a vector's component that way, such as v[i]. With a large array, loading can take minutes, and the player can then lose the GPU device and stop drawing. Compute the value instead, read the array at constant indices, or pick a component with a comparison, such as dot(v, float4(i == uint4(0, 1, 2, 3))). The same holds for HLSL in a Custom Function node.

On WebGL 2 in Chrome on Windows, a constant buffer that holds only one array of 50 or more elements counts as a texture. If a shader's textures and such buffers come to more than 16, the page stops drawing when that shader first draws. Add a second value after the array. Nothing has to read it.

A Sprite Without A Texture

Where a sprite has no normal map, mask, emission or Geometry texture, your shader samples the material's texture in that slot, or the property's default if the material has none. Unity's defaults draw such a sprite wrong, so set each slot:

Texture Property Set Otherwise the sprite
Normal map _NormalMap SunForge_FlatNormal.png on the material Lights as if tilted
Mask _MaskTex SunForge_NeutralMask.png on the material Lights as polished metal, or with the wrong metal, smoothness or occlusion
Emission _EmissionTex A default of Black Glows white
Geometry _GeometryTex A default of Black Takes the full height range
  • Normal map and mask: both textures are in the Runtime/Materials folder of the Sun Forge 2D Lighting package. In Shader Graph, set the property's Default to the texture, so every new material starts with it. In a hand-written shader, select the shader and set it under Default Maps in the Inspector.
  • Emission and Geometry: in Shader Graph, set the Texture 2D property's Mode to Black. A new property starts at White. In a hand-written shader, declare it as "black", such as _GeometryTex ("Geometry Texture", 2D) = "black" {}.

Check Setup reports a material with no texture on _NormalMap or _MaskTex, and its Fix binds the matching texture. It also reports a material whose _GeometryTex default, or the texture set on it, holds a height.

Geometry Textures

A Geometry texture holds each sprite pixel's height above the pivot in red, and in alpha where the sprite meets the ground. It is not a mesh or a position map. To paint one, see Lighting.md, Painting A Geometry Texture.

The Format

  • Height above the pivot is R * range * scale. Black is the pivot, white is the range, and nothing lies below the pivot.
  • range is the range a component on the renderer sets, where one does, and otherwise the material's Height Range (World Units). SFBakeHeightRange in SunForgeRendererBakeValues.hlsl reads it. scale is the object's scale.
  • A texel with alpha of 8/255 or more has a height. A texel below that has none: a standing sprite draws there as a flat upright card, and a ground sprite at height 0.
  • Red holds the height. Green and blue are reserved and no material reads them, so a gray map, a red-only map and a single-channel map with the same red read alike. Do not read green or blue in your own shader.
  • Save the map lossless, such as PNG. The texture is linear, uncrunched, and filtered as its sheet is.
  • Any texture format works that keeps red and moves alpha by at most 4/255. A compressed format keeps fewer heights, so Check Setup imports a painted map uncompressed.

Sun Forge's ground cover also reads alpha as the footprint's front edge: how far in front of the pivot the sprite meets the ground. Grass draws over the sprite only in front of that edge. If you don't use ground cover, ignore this table.

Alpha Front edge
243/255 to 255/255 At the pivot
242/255 down to 27/255 (n / 25)^2 of the range in front of the pivot, where n is (247 - a) / 9 rounded to the nearest whole number and a is alpha from 0 to 255
8/255 to 26/255 The full range in front of the pivot
Below 8/255 No height

To store an edge, set a to 247 - 9 * n, where n is 25 * sqrt(edge / range) rounded, at most 25, or to 255 when n is 0. Alpha up to 4/255 above or below the value you set reads the same edge.

To read a texel, use SFHeightShare, SFHeightAbsent and SFHeightFrontEdgeShare in SunForgeHeightDecode.hlsl, or SunForgeHeightTexel.TryDecode in C#.
