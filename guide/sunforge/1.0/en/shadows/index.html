Shadows

How sprites cast shadows, and how tree crowns shade what is under them.

On this page
  • Cast Shadows
  • Canopy Shade

Cast Shadows

Where the art is drawn from above, Set Up for Lighting sets your standing sprites up to cast shadows and adds a Cast Shadow Field, which draws them. The field's Mode sets where they fall.

How Dark A Shadow Is

A shadow sprite is as dark as you drew it under the Regular style's daylight with no cloud over it. It is darker under a sun stronger against its sky, as in the Realistic style, and fades as the sun or moon weakens against the sky, under cloud and under a lower sun. It also fades inside a Light Source's light, except on the Flat look and in a side view. To darken every shadow, raise Ground Shadow Strength on the Lighting Setup.

Cameras And Scripts

  • A Cast Shadow Field follows one camera, so in split screen both views share the shadows of the camera it follows.
  • The Cast Shadow Field draws once a frame, before the first camera. If a script moves the camera or a sprite between two renders in one frame, as a capture does, call its Redraw before the later render.
  • A caster whose shadow is far from the camera's view costs almost nothing. One whose Sprite Renderers are turned off costs a check of each of them every frame.

Flipped Sprites And Other Shaders

  • A Scale X of -1 on a parent, which turns a character to face left, does not mirror a paired shadow, so it still falls away from the sun.
  • Outside a Cast Shadow Field, a shadow sprite whose shader is not Sun Forge/Hand-Drawn Shadow mirrors with its parent and stays as dark as you drew it.
  • Mist does not cover particles, shadow sprites whose shader is not Sun Forge/Hand-Drawn Shadow, or shaders of your own that do not draw mist (Custom shaders › Shader Graph).

Canopy Shade

A Canopy Shade takes the sky's light off the ground and the sprites under tree crowns. To set one up:

  1. Choose GameObject > Sun Forge > Canopy Shade.
  2. Put your trees, and nothing else, under one object, with groups inside it if you like, and set the Canopy Shade's Trees to it.
  3. Set Rect to the ground the trees stand on.
  4. Click Draw Map. It saves the map beside the scene and sets Map and Canopy Height.

After you move, add or remove a tree, click Draw Map again. To choose how much of the shade shows, set Canopy Shade Strength on the Lighting Setup.

Paint The Map Yourself

Cover Rect with your map: white is open sky and black is under closed crowns. Draw each crown's shade 1 / sin e times as wide as it is deep, where e is the angle of the Lighting Setup's Art View, or 3 degrees where that is lower. The Art View is From Above wherever canopy shade shows.
