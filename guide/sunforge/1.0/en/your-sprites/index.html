Sprites you drew

Give a sprite you drew its normal map, its glow and its metal, and paint a Geometry texture for one that does not stand upright.

Sprite Textures

A sprite you drew gets its textures in one of two ways.

Have Sun Forge Write Them

  1. Once Set Up for Lighting has set the sprite up, select its object and open Glow, Surfaces and Edges on its Lighting Style Renderer.
  2. For each color that glows, click Add Glow Color, then click that color on the sprite.
  3. For each color that shines like metal, click Add Surface Color, then click that color on the sprite.
  4. Check the Glows and Shines pictures, which show the parts your colors match and gray out the rest. To match more, raise that color's Tolerance.
  5. For rounded edges, turn on Round Edges.
  6. Click Write Textures.
  7. For a glow that lights the ground and the sprite around it, click Add in the Glow Light row.

Bring Your Own

  1. Save each texture as a PNG beside the sprite's texture, named as in the table below, where <sheet> is that texture's file name without its extension.
  2. Run Set Up for Lighting, or click Change Setup on the sprite's Lighting Style Renderer.
  3. Under Sprites, the sprite's row names each file it found. Click Apply to give them to the sprite. A sprite that already has a texture of that kind keeps it.

You can also bind them under Secondary Textures in Unity's Sprite Editor.

What Each Texture Holds

A style's material reads these textures from any sprite that has them:

Material slot File Secondary texture name Channels
Normal Map <sheet>_Normal.png _NormalMap On Ground in a scene drawn from above, a normal map drawn top-down. Otherwise, one drawn from the same angle as your standing sprites. Fill its texels outside the sprite with the flat normal, 128, 128, 255, or the sprite's edge draws dark. Read it with SFUnpackNormal from SunForgeNormalDecode.hlsl, or the Sun Forge Sample Normal Map node in Shader Graph. Both read 128 as level
Mask <sheet>_MaskTex.png _MaskTex R metallic, G smoothness, B ambient occlusion, and on a sprite Sun Forge rendered, A how much sun reaches it. Linear
Emission <sheet>_Emission.png _EmissionTex The glow's color at intensity 1, sRGB, and on a texture Sun Forge rendered or wrote, A how much light the sprite takes from its own glow
Geometry Texture <sheet>_Geometry.png _GeometryTex R height above the pivot: black at the pivot, brighter is higher. A is optional (Geometry texture format). Linear and uncrunched

A sprite with a mask of another layout under _MaskTex lights wrong.

See The Textures In The Scene View

Turn on Texture View at the foot of the Sun Forge Gizmos panel in the Scene view and pick a texture.

  • The view shows only what a lit or unlit URP material draws, so sprites under Sprites/Default, cast shadows, lines and text hide.
  • Under Geometry, a texel with no height shows in dim gray, and under Emission, a sprite with no emission.

Painting A Geometry Texture

A sprite that stands upright needs no Geometry texture. Paint one for a sprite that is not an upright card, such as a table top, a cart or a figure lying down.

  1. Paint gray over every pixel the sprite covers: black at the ground, brighter the higher it stands. On a sheet, leave a sprite transparent to keep it an upright card.
  2. Put the sprite's pivot on its front edge where it meets the ground, such as a cart's front wheels. Grass draws over the sprite only in front of the pivot. For a sprite that stands on another, such as a lamp on a table, put the pivot where it meets the table, and set Height Above Ground on its Lighting Style Renderer to the table's height.
  3. Export a PNG named <sheet>_Geometry.png beside the sheet.
  4. Run Set Up for Lighting. The sprite's row names the texture, and Apply imports it and gives it to the sprite.
  • To work in pixels, gray is height in pixels * 255 / (Pixels Per Unit * range), where range is the material's Height Range (World Units). At 32 Pixels Per Unit and the default range of 8, one gray step is about one pixel.
  • A part that stands straight up, such as a chimney, and is N pixels tall in the drawing is N / cos(elevation) pixels high, where elevation is the angle of the Lighting Setup's Art View.
  • With Point filtering, nothing around the sprite matters. A filtered sheet needs the export's background transparent. Anything you leave transparent needs a PNG with alpha.
