Tune a style

Save a style as your own, set its look, and change its post-processing.

On this page
  • Look Rows
  • Post-Processing

To tune a look, save it as a style of your own:

  1. Select your Lighting Setup and open the section named for its style, below Values. On one of Sun Forge's styles, click Edit A Copy in the section's header. A style of your own takes edits as it is, and Save As New Style in the section's header saves a copy of it where you choose.
  2. Type a name for the copy in Name and press Enter. Its materials and post-processing take the name too.
  3. In Look, set the rows. Each row sets the style's Sprite, Ground and Ground Clutter materials together.
  4. To tune the post-processing, open Post Processing and edit the overrides under Post Processing Profile.

Look Rows

Each row's tooltip says what it does. These rows need more. Realistic's materials are Regular's with other values, so a row listed for Regular applies to Realistic too. Realistic has no Pixel Outline material.

Property Styles Note
Night Vision Strength All On Flat, start at 0.5
Highlight Ceiling Regular, Cinematic Red, green and blue each level off on their own, so a bright firelit glint turns from orange to gold to yellow. Without tonemapping, as under Regular, a glint above 1 turns to flat color
Pixel Outline, Pixel Outline Color Regular, Cinematic The ring draws only inside the sprite's rectangle, so leave a transparent pixel around the art, and on a sprite whose Mesh Type is Tight keep Extrude Edges at 1 or more. To outline only some sprites, set the Material of their Lighting Style Renderer to SunForge_Regular_SpriteLit_PixelOutline or SunForge_Cinematic_SpriteLit_PixelOutline, the shipped Sprite material with Pixel Outline on
Outline Width (World Units) Flat The default draws one sprite pixel at 64 Pixels Per Unit, and one and a half at 96. A sprite scaled down keeps the width, so a small copy looks more heavily outlined. Crease lines stay one sprite pixel wide
Light And Shade Color Flat Start at 0.5
Receives Cast Shadows, Bake Camera Elevation (Degrees), Height Range (World Units), Shadow Sun Elevation (Degrees), Shadow Sun Direction (Degrees) All Not in Look: select your style and click Open beside a material to set them on that material
Emission Scale All Not in Look: set it on each material, as above. It also brightens a sprite's Glow light (Lights › A Sprite's Own Glow Light)

Leave two transparent pixels around each sprite, so two sprites in one texture are four pixels apart. Where a sprite has fewer, Flat's outline and the rims of Regular and Cinematic are missing along that edge.

Post-Processing

The style's post-processing is its Volume profile, SunForge_<Style>_PostProcessing, which the Lighting Setup applies at priority 0.5.

  • A Volume of your own with a priority below 0.5 sets what the profile leaves out, such as a vignette on Flat. One at 1 or more overrides the style.
  • Regular and Flat turn tonemapping off, so a tonemapper set for the whole project does not show under them. Set it on a Volume with a priority of 1 or more instead.
  • The styles are tuned for the URP asset's Grading Mode at Low Dynamic Range, its default. At High Dynamic Range, which HDR display output also turns on, their nights are darker, and under Cinematic a fire's bright core turns a flat yellow.
