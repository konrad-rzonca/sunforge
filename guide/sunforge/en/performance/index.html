Performance

See what each lighting setting costs in your scene, and where to change it.

On this page
  • What Each Setting Costs
  • The First Frame In A Web Player

A setting that costs frame time shows a gauge after its name, and its tooltip says what it costs. The Lighting Setup's Performance section lists what costs in the open scenes, largest first, with Select beside each line.

To set the costly settings at once, open Performance and click Fastest, Balanced or Full, then tune any setting from there. Each button's tooltip says what it changes.

What Each Setting Costs

What each setting cost in a test scene, with and without it. A desktop is an RTX 4060, a phone a Pixel 8a. A range spans the readings. A desktop reading under about 0.8 ms is within the noise between runs.

On a desktop, 2026-11, Cloud Strength, Canopy Shade Strength and the Lighting Setup's Sprite Emission Enabled, Ambient Occlusion Enabled and Self Shadow Enabled cost nothing measurable. On a phone, 2026-10, cloud, canopy shade and ambient occlusion cost 1 ms or less each.

The First Frame In A Web Player

In a web player, the first frame that draws a lighting style, or a material under a style, can take seconds while the browser prepares its shaders: in Chrome on Windows, about 2 to 3 s on WebGPU and 7 to 13 s on WebGL 2. To spend that time behind your loading screen:

  1. Switch to each style your game uses and draw a sprite of every material under it, including those of inactive objects and of prefabs you spawn later.
  2. If a Sprite Skin animates any of your characters and GPU Skinning (Project Settings > Player) is not set to CPU, do this once with none of those characters active, then once with them active.
  3. If a style's Quality section draws an option at some quality levels and leaves it out at others, and your game changes its level while it runs, also draw that style at a level that draws the option and at one that leaves it out.
