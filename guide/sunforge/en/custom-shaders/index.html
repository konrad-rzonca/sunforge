Custom shaders

Start a Shader Graph from a Sun Forge template, or light a graph or hand-written sprite shader of your own with the scene's style, lights, shadows and mist.

On this page
  • Shader Graph
  • Hand-Written Shaders
  • A Sprite Without A Texture

Shader Graph

To start from a ready graph, choose Assets > Create > Shader Graph > From Template, pick Sun Forge Sprite Lit or Sun Forge Dissolve under Sun Forge, and do step 5 below. To build one yourself:

  1. In a Sprite Unlit graph, add Sun Forge > Sun Forge Sprite Lit.
  2. Connect its Color to Base Color and its Alpha to Alpha.
  3. In the Graph Settings, turn on Disable Color Tint. With it off, a sprite whose Sprite Renderer Color is black loses its glow, rim, mist and hit flash.
  4. Also in the Graph Settings, set Custom Editor GUI to KonradRzonca.SunForge.Editor.Inspector.SunForgeStyledMaterialEditor, so its material's Inspector shows the same preview and tooltips as a Sun Forge material's.
  5. Right-click the graph in the Project window and choose Create > Material. On each renderer that uses the material, choose Add Component > Sun Forge > Lighting > Lighting Style Renderer, and set its Material to your material. On a Mesh, Skinned Mesh, Line or Trail Renderer, set your textures on the material, not through a property block of your own, which can make the graph draw nothing.

The material then has the sprite's five texture slots and the material properties the lighting reads, as the Sun Forge materials have them. Set Role on the material to Sprite, Ground or Ground Clutter: the sprite takes that role's look, and its Lighting Style Renderer and the Cast Shadow Field take the role from the material.

To change the sprite before it is lit, such as a dissolve or a scrolling texture, use Sun Forge Sprite Textures and Sun Forge Lit Color in place of Sun Forge Sprite Lit. Connect each output of Sun Forge Sprite Textures to the Sun Forge Lit Color port of the same name, with your effect between them, and connect Sun Forge Lit Color as in step 2. Connect a coordinate to UV to sample the textures there.

Sun Forge Lit Color port Takes Left unconnected
Albedo The sprite's color and alpha White
Normal The sprite's normal, such as from Sun Forge Sample Normal Map Level ground on Ground with a Bake Camera Elevation (Degrees) above 0, else a surface facing the camera
Mask Metallic, smoothness and occlusion in red, green and blue: keep the alpha Sun Forge Sprite Textures gives No metal, half smoothness, no occlusion
Emission The glow color, and in alpha the light the sprite's own glow sends it: keep the alpha Sun Forge Sprite Textures gives, or set 1 for a color of your own No glow
Geometry The Geometry texture's sample (Geometry Textures, below) A standing sprite is a flat upright card

Ground cover, Flat's outlines, rims kept to the outline, a Point-filtered sprite's hard highlights and smooth light from a close Light Source under Regular and Cinematic read the sprite's own textures around each pixel. Where the graph draws a shape other than the sprite's own picture, set Shape From on Sun Forge Lit Color to This Graph, so they do not read the sprite's textures.

Node Gives
Sun Forge Sample Normal Map A normal map of your own, read as the Sun Forge materials read theirs, for the Normal port
Sun Forge Mist The scene's mist over a color, for an effect the Sun Forge nodes do not light
Sun Forge Scene Light The sun or moon's direction and color, the sky and ground colors the Lighting Setup publishes, and the time in seconds the Sun Forge materials animate by
  • A graph with a texture property of its own whose Reference is _MainTex, _NormalMap, _MaskTex, _EmissionTex or _GeometryTex shows an error once a Sun Forge node adds a slot of that Reference. Delete the property and take its sample from Sun Forge Sprite Textures.
  • A mesh that draws many small sprites at once, such as a shower of coins, lights each one at its own pivot and height through SunForgeBatchSprites. The node reads UV channel SunForgeBatchSprites.UvChannel for it, so keep your own data out of that channel.
  • A Sample Texture 2D, DDX, DDY or DDXY node between Color and Base Color can draw wrong colors along the sprite's edges on OpenGL and Vulkan. Sample such a texture with Sample Texture 2D LOD, or feed it to one of the Sun Forge Lit Color node's ports.
  • On a Sprite Skin, run the position plus the Tangent Vector through a second Sprite Skinning node, and connect that result minus the skinned position to the Tangent block, so the relief turns with the bone.
  • On WebGL 2 and OpenGL ES 3.0 a shader can sample 16 textures. The lighting samples 10 of its own, so a graph that uses Sun Forge Sprite Lit or Sun Forge Sprite Textures, which sample the sprite's five textures, can sample one more there.
  • On Direct3D a shader can use 16 sampler states. The lighting uses 6 of its own, so a graph that uses Sun Forge Sprite Lit or Sun Forge Sprite Textures can use five more. Textures sampled through one Sampler State node take one state between them.

Hand-Written Shaders

Each include is under Packages/com.konradrzonca.sunforge.lighting/Runtime/Shaders/, and its header says how to use it.

Include Use it to
SunForgeLitColor.hlsl Light a shader as the Sun Forge Lit Color node does.
SunForgeStyleKeywords.hlsl Give the shader a variant for each style, and a variant for each option of a style's Quality section (Styles › Style Quality). Include it with #include_with_pragmas beside the pass's pragmas.
SunForgeNormalDecode.hlsl Read a normal map sample as the shipped materials do, to light with it or to show or measure a normal yourself.
SunForgeHeightDecode.hlsl Declare a Geometry texture, and read the height and front edge of its sample, or the height of a cast height texture's, as the shipped materials do.
SunForgeHeightRamp.hlsl Color a height as the Texture View does under Geometry.
SunForgeHeightRampBar.hlsl Draw the legend's bar for that ramp in a pass named Height Ramp, which SunForgeHeightRamp.DrawBar runs.
SunForgeMist.hlsl Draw the scene's mist, such as in a full-screen fog.
SunForgeHitFlash.hlsl Draw the hit flash.
SunForgeBakedLight.hlsl Read, from an emission texture's alpha, the light a sprite's own glow sends it.
SunForgeWalkBands.hlsl Draw only your shader's piece of a prop that Sun Forge splits around a character.
SunForgeRendererBakeValues.hlsl Read the angle and the other projection values a sprite draws with, and how high it stands above the ground.
SunForgeLightingGlobals.hlsl Read what the Lighting Setup publishes.

To publish lighting of your own from a script, use SunForgeLightingPublisher.

On WebGPU, Chrome on Windows loads a shader slowly when it reads a static const array at an index computed at run time, or picks a vector's component that way, such as v[i]. With a large array, loading can take minutes, and the player can then lose the GPU device and stop drawing. Compute the value instead, read the array at constant indices, or pick a component with a comparison, such as dot(v, float4(i == uint4(0, 1, 2, 3))). The same holds for HLSL in a Custom Function node.

On WebGL 2 in Chrome on Windows, a constant buffer that holds only one array of 50 or more elements counts as a texture. If a shader's textures and such buffers come to more than 16, the page stops drawing when that shader first draws. Add a second value after the array. Nothing has to read it.

A Sprite Without A Texture

Where a sprite has no normal map, mask, emission or Geometry texture, your shader samples the material's texture in that slot, or the property's default if the material has none. The slots Sun Forge Sprite Textures adds already have the right defaults. In a hand-written shader, Unity's defaults draw such a sprite wrong, so set each slot:

Texture Property Set Otherwise the sprite
Normal map _NormalMap SunForge_FlatNormal.png as its default Lights as if tilted
Mask _MaskTex SunForge_NeutralMask.png as its default Lights as polished metal, or with the wrong metal, smoothness or occlusion
Emission _EmissionTex "black" Glows white
Geometry _GeometryTex "black" Takes the full height range
  • Normal map and mask: both textures are in the Runtime/Materials folder of the Sun Forge 2D Lighting package. Select the shader and set them under Default Maps in the Inspector.
  • Emission and Geometry: declare the property as "black", such as _GeometryTex ("Geometry Texture", 2D) = "black" {}.

Check Setup reports a material with no texture on _NormalMap or _MaskTex, and its fix binds the matching texture. It also reports a material whose _GeometryTex holds a height.

A Shader Of Your Own

Build the graph on the Sun Forge Sprite Lit node, or on Sun Forge Sprite Textures and Sun Forge Lit Color (Custom shaders › Shader Graph). Then use it one of two ways:

  • On a baked sprite. Add a Lighting Style Renderer beside its Baked Renderer Binding (Add Component > Sun Forge > Lighting > Lighting Style Renderer) and set its Material to your material. The sprite keeps its bake's values.
  • As the bake's material. Pick the material as the bake's Prefab Material (Advanced view). The bake sets the sprite's bake values, such as its Bake Camera Elevation (Degrees), and the prefab needs no Lighting Style Renderer.

A baked effect's particles add their light to what is behind them whatever their alpha:

  • A graph whose Blending Mode is Premultiply, with Premultiplied connected to Base Color, draws that light from the effect's emission texture. With another Blending Mode, the light bakes into the effect's color.
  • Under a style whose Sprite material cannot draw it, a baked effect's Particles child keeps the material it was baked with.
  • A hand-written shader draws that light if it declares _SFEmissionScale and _SFEmissionOutsideAlpha and blends One OneMinusSrcAlpha. The header of SunForgeLitColor.hlsl shows how.
