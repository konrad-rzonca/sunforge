All features 2D lighting

Real-time 2D lighting for Unity URP

Sun Forge 2D Lighting lights a 2D or 2.5D scene like 3D, with a sun, a moon and a sky from day to night, lights, cast shadows and four styles. It lights baked sprites and sprites you drew.

Drag the star to take the light off the hall, and pick a texture to see what the light reads.

A real-time sun, from day to night

A sun, a moon and a sky light the scene by day and by night. Change the time of day by hand, from a script or with an animation, and the sprites relight with nothing baked again.

At night, moonlit surfaces turn blue while firelight and glows keep their color.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek and the skeleton by LasquetiSpice, CC BY 4.0, modified.

Dynamic 2D lights that flicker like fire

A Light Source can be a point, a spot, a line or a rectangle, and can flicker like fire. In the Scene view, handles set each light's height, reach and spot angles.

A Unity Light 2D can drive a Light Source, and it keeps lighting Sun Forge's Unity Lights material.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek and the skeleton by LasquetiSpice, CC BY 4.0, modified.

2D shadows that fall on other sprites

Shadows move with their sprites, fall on the ground and on other sprites, and fade as the sun sets. A cast shadow climbs the sprites standing in it, as high as what casts it. Drag the star to compare.

Shadows fall in the sun direction they were baked or drawn for, and do not turn as the sun moves.

Ground mist and cloud shadows

The sky adds drifting cloud shadows and ground mist. The mist covers each sprite from the feet up, because each baked sprite stores its height. Every Light Source lights the mist too.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek and the skeleton by LasquetiSpice, CC BY 4.0, modified.

Four lighting styles to start from

Regular, Cinematic, Flat and Realistic light the same sprites, and the style changes live, in Play too. You can tune a style and save it as your own.

Each style has its own settings, such as a pixel outline in Regular and Cinematic, painted light in Cinematic, and colors that turn gold at sunset in Flat. Flat draws outlines and crease lines.

The Forgotten Knight by dark_igorek and the skeleton by LasquetiSpice, CC BY 4.0, modified.

Light sprites you drew, pixel art too

Select your sprites, right-click and choose Sun Forge > Set Up for Lighting. A normal map of your own gives them form, and without one a sprite is lit as a flat surface. You pick the colors that glow and the colors that shine like metal, and Sun Forge writes their glow and metal textures.

More about the lighting

URP's 2D Renderer and Universal Renderer

The lighting works under both, and its materials need no shader work.

Set up from one menu item

A scene's lighting sets up from one menu item and Fix All.

Live in the editor and in a player

The lighting draws live in Edit mode, Play mode and a player, so the sun or a light that a script or an animation changes relights the sprites.

Metal, smoothness and ambient occlusion

The lighting reads each sprite's normal, mask and Geometry textures. The mask holds metal in red, smoothness in green and ambient occlusion in blue.

Platforms

Sun Forge is built for every platform URP supports. Players run on Windows (Direct3D 12), on Android (ARM64) with Vulkan and with OpenGL ES 3, on WebGL 2, and on WebGPU with Unity 6.6.

Without the sprite baker

Each tool works without the other. The lighting lights sprites you drew in any 2D or 2.5D scene, top-down, isometric or side view, seen by a camera that faces the sprites straight on.

Bake the sprites it lights

One purchase holds Sun Forge 2D Lighting and the sprite baker, which writes the textures the lighting reads. See all features, or read its guide.
